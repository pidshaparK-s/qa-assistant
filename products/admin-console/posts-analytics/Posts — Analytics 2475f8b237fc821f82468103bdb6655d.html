A1: Context

Feedback from many customers, including Ulta, David Lloyd, XM, and many others — has highlighted limited visibility into how their Social+ content performs, and how different audiences interact with that content.

As we evolve toward a more componentized, in-app engagement infrastructure, our analytics needs to evolve with it. Social+ is no longer only a destination for communities; its components can be embedded across different surfaces and experiences within a customer's application.

This creates an opportunity to provide customers with a broader understanding of their Social+ ecosystem — not only what content is being created and how it performs, but also how different audiences consume, engage with, and contribute to that content.

We looked at established patterns in marketing, social media, and content analytics dashboards. These products commonly provide a high-level channel performance view, followed by deeper content and audience analysis.

From an analytics perspective, Social+ posts can be treated similarly to a channel within the customer's product: posts are created, distributed across destinations, consumed, and engaged with. This makes a channel-level view useful for understanding the overall performance of Posts as an engagement component, before drilling down into the content driving that performance.

Based on this, Post Analytics is structured around two distinct analytical questions:

  1. Channel Performance: How are Social+ posts performing as an engagement component?
  1. Content Performance: Which posts are performing, and what’s driving that performance?

Audience Intelligence

We initially considered including some audience intelligence within Post Analytics, as understanding who is consuming and engaging with posts is a natural extension of content performance. However, as we shaped the experience further, we recognized that the underlying problem was broader: customers want to understand what their audiences are interested in and how the behave across their Social+ experience.

That interest may be expressed through posts, but also through comments, reactions, comments, live streams, and other interactions. Keeping Audience Intelligence within each component would constrain the solution to a single component and ultimate require audience analysis to be duplicated across components.

We therefore decided to treat Audience Intelligence as a broader, cross-component capability, focusing on building an overall behavioral profile of an audience from signals across Social+. As part of this, customers will receive aggregate audience insights as part of their existing Social+ analytics experience, allowing them to discover the value of their audience data. For example, they may learn which audience segments are most engaged or what content they respond to across Social+.

Customers who want to identify the specific users behind these insights and take action on those audiences can unlock this through a paid module.

This creates a clear product-led growth journey:

Show the value → Identify the audience → Take action

A2: MVP Principles & Boundaries

  • Channel Performance should follow familiar marketing/channel analytics patterns: headline KPIs, trends, synthesized insights, and lightweight evidence.
  • Content Performance should provide deeper investigation of individual Posts and their performance drivers.
  • Keep the MVP focused and avoid building a flexible analytics/query engine.
  • Prioritize trustworthy and actionable insights over breadth.
  • [Added 22-Sept]: Analytics and Operations should be treated as separate experiences, with Analytics focusing on understanding performance, and Operations focused on managing content and taking action.
    • Our customers use Social+ in different organizational contexts. For larger enterprise customers (e.g. True, AirBnB, Ulta), we see separate teams responsible for day-to-day content/community operations and performance analysis and reporting. These users have different jobs: operational users need to manage and take action on content, while analytical users need to understand performance, identify trends, and investigate what is driving results.
    • Smaller customers may have the same person performing both roles, but the underlying jobs remain distinct.
    • Therefore, Analytics and Operations should be designed as separate experiences around their distinct jobs, with Analytics focused on understanding and investigating performance, and Operations focused on managing content and taking action. The two experiences can remain connected where useful, but Analytics should not replicate operational workflows.

User Stories / Journeys

Understand Overall Performance of S+ Posts

As a marketer, I want to understand the overall performance of my Social+ posts and quickly identify the most important trends, content, and audience signals within my selected scope.

Page Structure:

The Overview page consists of:

  • Key Insights — synthesized findings from the selected scope
  • Key Post Metrics — high-level measure of post activity and performance
  • Post Performance over Time — changes in key performance metrics over the selected period
  • Top Performing Posts — posts contributing strongly to overall performance

Analytical Scope:

The user can define the scope of their analysis using:

  • Date filter — the period over which post activity and performance is analyzed
  • Destination — the destinations associated with the posts included in the analysis
  • Creator role — the creator roles associated with the posts included in the analysis

All widgets in the Overview page should reflect the selected scope

1. Post Overview — Filters

As a marketer, I want to filter my analysis by date, destination, and creator role so that I can focus on the Post activity that is relevant to me.
  • User should be able to define the scope of the Overview using the following filters:
    • Date
      • User can select a date range
      • Selected date range applies to all Overview widgets
      • On the Overview page, the date range represents the activity period
      • Post activity and performance metrics should include activity occurring within the selected period
    • Destination
      • The user can select a destination.
      • Selecting a destination includes Posts associated with that destination.
      • A Post associated with multiple destinations should be included when any of its associated destinations matches the selected filter.
    • Creator role
      • User can select one or more creator roles, from admin, moderator, and user role
      • Selecting a creator role includes posts created by users with that role

2. View synthesized insights for posts

As a marketer, I want to see the most important insights about my Post performance so that I can quickly understand what is happening and identify areas I may want to investigate further.
  • The Overview should display 3 key insight cards
  • Insights should be dynamically selected from a predefined insight library rather than being permanently assigned to specific cards
  • The insights displayed should be relevant to the user’s selected date, destination, and creator role filters
  • Each insight should communicate one clear finding rather than requiring the user to interpret a chart or multiple metrics
  • Insight statements should be specific about what is driving the finding and, where relevant, include the supporting metric or value.
  • Insights should provide enough context for the user to understand why the finding is notable.
  • The three cards should aim to provide different perspectives on Post performance rather than repeating the same underlying finding.
  • Where an insight can be investigated further, the card should provide a clear path to the relevant deeper analysis.
  • The insight should be worded according to the selected scope. For example, when a destination is selected, the insight should make clear that the finding relates to Posts within that destination scope where necessary.
  • If there are not enough meaningful insights to populate all three cards, fewer cards may be displayed rather than showing irrelevant or repetitive insights.

Example:

Video drives the most engagement

Video Posts generated 48% of engagement despite representing 22% of Posts.

View content performance →

Beauty Enthusiasts drive the most engagement

Beauty Enthusiasts generated 34% of engagement with Posts.

View audience →

Post engagement is increasing

Engagement increased 24% compared with the previous period.

View performance over time →

3. View Key Post Metrics

As a marketer, I want to see key post performance metrics at a glance so that I can quickly understand the scale and overall performance of my posts.
  • Display the following metrics:
    • Posts published
    • Total reach
    • Total impressions
    • Total comments
    • Total reactions
    • Engaged users
    • Engagement rate (%)
  • Metrics reflect the selected global filters
  • Where applicable, display a comparison against the previous equivalent period
  • If the selected scope contains insufficient data to calculate a metric, display an appropriate unavailable state rather than a misleading value
    • If engagement data is unavailable because engagement functionality is not enabled for the Posts within the selected scope, display an appropriate unavailable state rather than 0.
    • A value of 0 should only be displayed when engagement functionality is available and no eligible engagement actions occurred during the selected period.
    • Potential metrics the above handling may apply to:
      • Total comments
      • Total reactions
      • Engaged users
      • Engagement rate (%)

4. View Post Performance over Time

As a marketer, I want to understand how post performance changes over time so that I can identify trends and changes in content creation, reach, impressions, and engagement.
  • Display post performance over the selected date range from the global filter
  • Each data point represents the selected metric for the corresponding time interval.
  • Metrics are calculated based on the timestamp of the underlying activity, rather than when the post was published
    • For example, if the filter selected was September, if a post published in August receives impressions in September, those impressions contribute to the September data point
  • The time interval displayed should adapt to the selected date range
  • Users can select which metric to visualize from the available post performance metrics, which are:
    • Posts created (Default)
    • Reach
    • Impressions
    • Comments
    • Reactions
    • Engaged Users
    • Engagement Rate (%)
  • The selected global filters apply to the time-series data
  • When a metric is not available for the selected scope, display the appropriate unavailable state rather than interpreting unavailable data as zero

5. View Top Performing Posts

As a marketer, I want to see which posts are resonating with my audience and gaining traction so that I can understand what content is currently driving audience interest and engagement.
  • Display the top X posts (TBD)
  • Posts are ranked based on activity generated during the selected date range, regardless of when the post was originally published.
    • A post published before the selected period can still appear if it generated significant activity during the period
    • This keep the widget aligned with the activity-based nature of the Overview
  • Allow users to sort by reach, impressions, comments, reactions, engaged users, and engagement rate (%)
  • Performance metrics shown in the widget represent activity generated during the selected period, not lifetime performance.
  • All selected global filters apply
  • Users can select a Post to open its Post Detail view.
    • Post Detail shows both:
      • Selected-period performance — activity during the Overview's selected date range
      • Lifetime performance — activity since the Post was published

6. Export Post Analytics

As a marketer, I want to export my post performance data so that I can perform further analysis and reporting outside of Social+
  • Provide an export button on the Overview page
  • Selecting export exports the analytics data for the current analysis scope. The export should respect the currently selected global filters:
    • Date filter
    • Destination
    • Creator role
  • The export should contain two datasets:
    • Post performance over time (all metrics, regardless of which metric the user selected on the chart)
    • Top performing posts
  • Dataset 1 — Post performance over time
    • The export should contain the aggregated data represented by the performance over time chart for all metrics, regardless of which metrics is selected in the UI
    • Example:
  • Dataset 2 — Top Post performance
    • The export should contain post-level performance data for posts that generated activity based on the global filters selected. It should include all qualifying Posts, not only the top 5 or 10 Posts displayed in the widget.
    • Each row represents one post
    • Performance metrics represent activity generated by each Post during the selected period, rather than lifetime performance.
    • Include the same post attributes and performance metrics displayed in the Top Performing Posts widget.

Understand Content Performance of S+ Posts

As a marketer, I want to investigate what’s driving my post performance so that I can understand which content, destinations, and creators are contributing to performance.

Page Structure:

The Content Performance page consists of:

  • Performance by Post Type — contribution of different post types to overall post performance
  • Destination Performance — post performance across different publishing destinations
  • All Posts — individual post performance
  • Top Creators — creator contribution to post activity and performance

Analytical Scope:

The user can define the scope of their analysis using:

  • Date filter — the post creation date
  • Destination — the destinations associated with the posts included in the analysis
  • Creator role — the creator roles associated with the posts included in the analysis
  • Post type — filters to posts of the selected post type(s) (e.g. text, image, video, etc.)

All widgets in the Content Performance page should reflect the selected scope

1. Post Overview — Filters

As a marketer, I want to filter my analysis by date, destination, creator role, and post type so that I can focus on the posts that are relevant to me.
  • Users can filter Content Performance by:
    • Post Creation Date
    • Destination
    • Creator Role (admin, moderator, user)
    • Post Type (text, image, video, audio, file, mixed)
  • Filters apply across all Content Performance widgets.
  • Performance metrics for included Posts represent their lifetime performance, regardless of when that performance occurred.
Important date semantics: Content Performance uses publication date to define the post cohort, while its performance metrics reflect the lifetime performance (from creation date until T) of posts in that cohort.

For example, if the user selects Sep 1–15:

  • A Post published Sep 5 → included, lifetime performance shown.
  • A Post published Aug 20 → excluded.
  • A Post published Sep 5 that continues receiving activity in October → October activity is still included in its lifetime metrics.

2. View Performance by Post Type

As a marketer, I want to compare performance across content types so that I can understand which types of content are contributing most to post performance.
  • Display performance by post type in a list / table format
  • Display:
    • Posts created
    • Reach
    • Impressions
    • Comments
    • Reactions
    • Engaged Users
    • Engagement Rate (%)
  • Users can sort the list by any of the data points above
  • Users can export the data represented in the widget → Export the aggregated Post performance data by Post Type based on the selected filters. Each row represents a Post Type and includes the metrics displayed in the widget.
  • Users can view all posts → view the individual posts contributing to the post types and their performance

Why lists instead of a chart?

Post Type and Destination are both filters and analytical dimensions. A chart becomes less useful when the user filters to a single value, as the comparison collapses to a single data point. A list/table maintains a consistent structure whether the user is comparing multiple Post Types or viewing a single selected Post Type, while also allowing multiple performance metrics to be displayed and sorted.

[Appendix A] Insight Library

The system should maintain a library of predefined insight types that can be surfaced on the Post Overview.

The initial library should include:

InsightDescriptionExample
Top ContentIdentifies the individual Post generating the most engagement.“Post X generated the most engagement.”
Post Type LeaderIdentifies the content type generating the most engagement.“Video generated the most engagement.”
Content MixIdentifies content types whose share of engagement differs meaningfully from their share of Posts.“Video represents 22% of Posts but drives 48% of engagement.”
Destination LeaderIdentifies the destination generating the most engagement.“Product Page generated the most engagement.”
Destination MixIdentifies destinations whose share of engagement differs meaningfully from their share of Posts.“Product Page accounts for 30% of Posts but 52% of engagement.”
Engagement DriverIdentifies the engagement action contributing the largest share of engagement.“68% of engagement came from reactions.”
Growth / DeclineIdentifies a meaningful change in Post performance compared with a previous period.“Post engagement increased 24% vs. the previous period.”
Audience DriverIdentifies the audience segment (based on customer-defined user tags) generating the most engagement with Posts in the selected scope.“Beauty Enthusiasts generated the most engagement with Posts.”

The library should be extensible so that additional insight types can be introduced without changing the overall Key Insights experience.

A.1 Insight Selection

  • The system should evaluate available insight types against the current filter state and underlying data.
  • The system should select up to 3 eligible insights.
  • Insights should be selected based on eligibility, significance, and analytical diversity.
  • The position of an insight card should not be permanently tied to a specific insight type.
  • If an insight is not eligible, another eligible insight should be selected where available.
  • If fewer than 3 insights are eligible, the system should return only the eligible insights.

A.2 Insight Eligibility

An insight is eligible only when it provides a meaningful finding within the selected scope.

An insight should not be surfaced when:

  • The selected filters already determine the insight, making it redundant.
  • There is insufficient data to support the finding.
  • The underlying metric cannot be reliably calculated.
  • The insight substantially duplicates another selected insight.
  • The relevant analytical dimension has been collapsed by the selected filters.
  • Exclude insights that might discourage the customer

Example:

If Destination = Product Page, the system should not surface:

Product Page generated the most engagement.

because there is no longer a destination comparison.

However, the system may surface:

Video generated the most engagement with Posts associated with Product Page.

A.3 Insight Diversity

When selecting multiple insights, the system should prioritize distinct analytical dimensions.

Where sufficient eligible insights exist, the system should avoid selecting multiple insights that communicate essentially the same finding.

For example, the following should not normally appear together:

  • Video generated the most engagement.
  • Video had the highest engagement rate.
  • Video generated 48% of total engagement.

Instead, the system should prioritize a combination of different dimensions, such as:

  • Content
  • Distribution
  • Engagement
  • Audience
  • Momentum

The exact prioritization order is defined separately.

A.4 Filter Scope

Insights generated within Post Overview should respect the page's global filters:

  • Date / Time Period
  • Destination
  • Creator Role

The system should ensure that the insight statement accurately represents the resulting scope.

For example:

Creator Role = Admin

Video generated the most engagement among Admin-created Posts.

Destination = Product Page

Beauty Enthusiasts generated the most engagement with Posts associated with Product Page.

The Audience Driver insight is Post-scoped within this context. It describes the audience engaging with the Posts included in the current Post Overview scope; it does not represent overall audience behavior across Social+.

A.5 Data Traceability

Every synthesized insight must be supported by underlying data.

  • The system must be able to identify the data used to generate the insight.
  • Where the insight relates to specific Posts, those Posts should be available for further investigation.
  • Where the insight relates to a dimension such as content type or destination, the corresponding breakdown should be available in Content Performance.
  • Where the insight relates to an audience, the relevant audience analysis should be available through Audience Intelligence.

This ensures:

Insight → Evidence → Investigation

A.6 Insight Wording

Insight statements should:

  • Clearly identify the metric or dimension being highlighted.
  • Avoid vague claims such as "best" or "top-performing" without specifying the measure.
  • Avoid subjective or evaluative language.
  • Reflect the selected analytical scope.
  • Use specific values where they help explain why the finding is notable.
  • Avoid implying causation where the underlying data only demonstrates correlation or association.

Preferred:

Product Page generated the most engagement.

Avoid:

Product Page is your top-performing destination.

A.7 Insufficient Data

  • If there is insufficient data to generate an insight, the insight should not be displayed.
  • If no meaningful insights are available, the Key Insights section should display an appropriate empty state.
  • The system should not generate generic insights solely to fill an available card slot.
